\begin{brecipe}[the filled-in applications --- three rules, three
targets]\label{rec:sb-filled}
\[
\mathsf{avoid}\,\beta \;:=\; \mathsf{G}\neg\beta
\qquad\qquad
\mathsf{GF}\varphi \;:=\; \mathsf{Streett}((\top, \varphi))
\qquad\qquad
\mathsf{FG}\neg\beta \;:=\; \mathsf{Streett}((\beta, \bot))
\]
Each rule\textquotesingle s concluding judgement discharges through one
existing rule: \rulename{SugarAvoid} through the safety battery
(\rulename{QSafetyBattery}) at the
avoid set $[\![\beta]\!]$, with $\neg\beta$ read by the semantic
negation clause $[\![\neg\beta]\!] = S \smallsetminus [\![\beta]\!]$
(the \rulename{NNFDuality} atom dualities supply the syntactic normal
form --- the atom swap alone when $\beta$ is an atom, De Morgan
propagation when it is compound); \rulename{SugarGF} through the
degenerate bundle (\rulename{StreettDegenerate}); \rulename{SugarFG}
through one \rulename{QStreettBundle} node at the pair
$(\beta, \bot)$.
\end{brecipe}

\begin{bprop}[the exactness identities]\label{prop:sb-exact}
(i) Avoidance (\rulename{SugarAvoid}): a path always avoids
$[\![\beta]\!]$ iff it always
satisfies $\neg\beta$ --- definitional, by the negation clause
$[\![\neg\beta]\!] = S \smallsetminus [\![\beta]\!]$, with
\rulename{NNFDuality} normalizing the syntax where needed.
(ii) Recurrence (\rulename{SugarGF}): the pair $(\top, \varphi)$ holds iff
$\varphi$-states occur infinitely often (the degeneration identity,
proved as Proposition 4 of the \rulename{StreettDegenerate} section).
(iii) Eventual permanence (\rulename{SugarFG}): the pair $(\beta, \bot)$ holds iff
$\beta$-states occur only finitely often, i.e.\ iff eventually the path
permanently satisfies $\neg\beta$:
\[
\bigl(\exists^\infty n,\ \omega_n \in [\![\beta]\!]\bigr) \Rightarrow
\bigl(\exists^\infty n,\ \omega_n \in \varnothing\bigr)
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\neg\bigl(\exists^\infty n,\ \omega_n \in [\![\beta]\!]\bigr) ,
\]
since the consequent is false outright --- two lines: an implication
into falsehood is the negation of its hypothesis, and finitely many
$\beta$-visits along a discrete path is precisely a tail of
$\neg\beta$-states.
\end{bprop}
\begin{proof}
As displayed; only (iii) has any content, and its two lines are shown.
\end{proof}

\begin{bprop}[soundness corollary]\label{prop:sb-sound}
Each sugared judgement is concluded by its target rule's theorem, with
no additional obligation: the sugar constructor builds the expansion,
so the sugared judgement \emph{is} the target judgement.
\end{bprop}
\begin{proof}
Definitional unfolding plus the target sections' theorems. The witness
that the surface implementation honours the definitions --- that sugar
constructors build expansions rather than introducing new node kinds ---
is the reference implementation's concrete-syntax canonicality test
battery; no checker-facing artefact depends on it, since no downstream
representation contains a sugar node.
\end{proof}

\begin{bremark}[why $\mathsf{FG}$ costs a bundle and $\mathsf{GF}$ costs
only its degenerate case]
The two dual sugars land at different points of the same rule.
$\mathsf{GF}\varphi$ requests always and so erases the request
machinery; $\mathsf{FG}\neg\beta$ makes $\beta$ itself the request and
forbids response --- the ranking certificate must then pay out at every
$\beta$-visit with nothing ever refunding it, which is exactly a proof
that $\beta$-visits are finitely many. That both directions are single
nodes of one rule --- where automaton-primitive designs pay a product
construction for one of them --- is the practical content of the
Streett-primitive choice (see the \rulename{QStreettBundle} section's
remark).
\end{bremark}
